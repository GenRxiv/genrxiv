# GenRxiv: An Open Archive for AI-Generated Research

**A short technical paper on the goals, purpose, and architecture of GenRxiv.**

---

## Abstract

GenRxiv is a preprint archive built specifically for research that was
substantially generated or co-generated by artificial intelligence. Where
existing preprint servers (arXiv, bioRxiv, ChemRxiv) were designed for
human-authored work and are still working out how to handle AI-generated
submissions, GenRxiv starts from the opposite assumption: AI involvement is
the default, and the author's job is to disclose it plainly. Submissions are
accepted in Markdown only, authors are identified by ORCID iD, every paper is
released under CC0, and the archive is engineered to be readable by both
people and machines. This paper describes what GenRxiv is for, what it is
trying to accomplish, and how it is built.

---

## 1. Introduction

Preprint servers are a fixture of modern research communication. arXiv serves
physics and mathematics; bioRxiv and medRxiv serve the life sciences;
ChemRxiv serves chemistry. Each was built around the assumptions of its
field: a particular submission format, a particular review or moderation
model, and a particular idea of what an "author" is.

None of them were designed with AI-generated work in mind, and most are still
working out what to do about it. The result is an awkward situation: authors
produce research with the help of large language models, but have no
dedicated venue in which to deposit that work with the AI involvement
disclosed openly. Some hide it; some are forced into human-authored framing
that does not match how the work was actually produced.

GenRxiv starts from the other end. It assumes AI involvement and asks authors
to disclose it plainly, so the work can be read, cited, and evaluated with
that context in the open rather than guessed at. It is not peer-reviewed and
does not evaluate the scientific quality of submissions. Community
endorsement — upvotes by other ORCID-identified authors — is the signal
layer, not editorial gatekeeping. The source code for the platform is
available publicly [@genrxiv_repo], and the live archive is at genrxiv.org
[@genrxiv_site].

## 2. Goals and Purpose

GenRxiv has a small number of clear goals.

### 2.1 A home for AI-generated research

The primary goal is to provide a dedicated, open archive for research that
was substantially generated or co-generated by AI. Authors submit work
knowing that AI involvement is the expected condition of the venue, not an
exception to be argued for.

### 2.2 Honest disclosure as a first-class requirement

Authors must confirm that they reviewed and verified AI-generated content,
and every paper includes an "AI Involvement" section describing what parts of
the work were AI-generated or co-generated and with what tools. Disclosure is
not optional metadata tucked into a footnote; it is part of the document
itself.

### 2.3 Verifiable authorship

Author identity is established exclusively through ORCID iD [@orcid]. There are no
email/password accounts. Attribution is therefore verifiable: an ORCID iD
resolves to a real person, and the same person can be recognized across
submissions and across endorsements.

### 2.4 Machine readability by default

Because submissions are Markdown rendered to structured HTML, every paper is
immediately parseable by search engines, indexing services, and AI tools —
no PDF extraction required. The archive additionally exposes OAI-PMH 2.0
metadata [@oai_pmh], a sitemap, an Atom feed [@atom], schema.org JSON-LD
[@schema_org], a public stats API, and an AI plugin manifest for agent
discovery. The archive is meant to be read by agents as well as by people.

### 2.5 Open licensing

All submissions are released under CC0 1.0 (Public Domain Dedication)
[@cc0]. There is no license negotiation, no paywall, and no restricted
access tier.

### 2.6 Community signal instead of editorial gatekeeping

GenRxiv does not peer-review submissions. Moderation is a thin layer that
checks format and disclosure, not scientific merit. The signal layer is
endorsement: ORCID-identified authors can endorse papers they find useful,
and endorsement counts are public. This keeps the archive open while still
providing a quality signal that is itself attributable.

### 2.7 Space-efficient storage

GenRxiv stores the Markdown source, not a rendered PDF. Papers are rendered
to HTML with KaTeX for math and native SVG for figures; a downloadable PDF is
generated on demand, but the HTML is the version of record. This keeps
storage at roughly 50–200 KB per paper instead of the 2–10 MB typical of
PDF-first preprint servers — a 20–50x reduction — and makes every paper
machine-readable by construction.

## 3. Submission and Authoring

Authors prepare papers by pasting a published author prompt into any LLM
along with their research content. The prompt produces a Markdown document
with LaTeX math (`$...$`), SVG figures, an AI-involvement disclosure, a CC0
license statement, and exactly three OECD Fields of Science subject
classifications. The resulting document is ready to submit as-is.

The authoring format is deliberately constrained:

- **Markdown only.** No YAML front matter, no raw HTML, no LaTeX document
  commands. Plain Markdown.
- **LaTeX math notation** inside dollar signs, rendered by KaTeX [@katex].
- **SVG preferred for figures.** Raster images are accepted but capped at
  500 KB each and 2 MB total per submission.
- **Exactly three OECD Fields of Science classifications** per paper, drawn
  from a published taxonomy [@oecd_fos] exposed at `/api/fos`.

The submission workflow is deliberately simple: submit, moderate, publish.
Authors are notified by email when their submission is approved or rejected.

## 4. Architecture

GenRxiv is built on a purpose-built FastAPI application [@fastapi] with
PostgreSQL [@postgresql]. There is no OJS, no Apache, no PHP. The entire
stack is containerized and deployed via Docker Compose.

### 4.1 Component overview

```
                    Cloudflare Tunnel
                           |
                        nginx
                           |
        +------------------+------------------+
        |                  |                  |
    FastAPI API    Conversion Service    PostgreSQL
    (submission,   (Markdown → HTML/PDF   (articles, authors,
     moderation,    via Pandoc + Tectonic)  downloads, sessions,
     articles,      No published ports      endorsements, settings,
     OAI-PMH,                               schema_migrations)
     auth, stats,
     agent discovery,
     maintenance mode)
        |
     Article files
     (HTML, PDF renders)
```

The four services are:

1. **FastAPI API** — the application core. Handles submission, moderation,
   article serving, OAI-PMH, authentication, stats, agent discovery, and
   maintenance mode. Python 3.12, exposed only to nginx.
2. **Conversion service** — a sandboxed sidecar that renders author-submitted
   Markdown to HTML and PDF using Pandoc [@pandoc] and Tectonic [@tectonic].
   It has no published ports and is reachable only from the API container.
3. **PostgreSQL 16** [@postgresql] — the system of record for articles,
   authors, downloads, sessions, endorsements, settings, and schema
   migrations. Access is via psycopg3 [@psycopg] with a connection pool.
4. **nginx** — the reverse proxy, terminating HTTP and applying rate limits
   and security headers in front of the API.

A Cloudflare Tunnel [@cloudflare_tunnel] exposes the nginx service to the
public internet without opening any inbound ports on the host.

### 4.2 The API application

The FastAPI application is organized into focused modules:

| Module | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `api/main.py` | App construction, middleware, router mounting |
| `api/config.py` | Configuration dataclass loaded from environment |
| `api/db.py` | psycopg3 connection pool, schema definition, settings helpers |
| `api/auth.py` | ORCID OAuth, sessions, `require_author` / `require_admin` |
| `api/articles.py` | Submission, viewing, moderation, endorsements, stats, versioning, maintenance |
| `api/web.py` | HTML pages: browse, submit, dashboard, admin |
| `api/oai.py` | OAI-PMH 2.0 endpoint |
| `api/sitemap.py` | Sitemap, robots.txt, Atom feed |
| `api/notifications.py` | Email notifications on approve/reject |
| `api/ratelimit.py` | SlowAPI rate limiter configuration |
| `api/migrate.py` | SQL migration runner |

All SQL uses parameterized queries with psycopg3 [@psycopg] `%s` placeholders
— no string interpolation. Authentication is handled through three dependency
tiers: `get_current_author` (optional), `require_author` (401 if not logged
in), and `require_admin` (403 if not an admin ORCID).

### 4.3 Database schema

The schema is defined in `api/db.py` and created automatically on API
startup via `init_schema()`. It consists of eight tables:

- `authors` — ORCID-identified authors
- `articles` — submitted and published papers
- `article_authors` — many-to-many authorship
- `downloads` — download tracking
- `endorsements` — community signal
- `sessions` — authenticated sessions
- `settings` — key/value site settings (including the maintenance flag)
- `schema_migrations` — migration tracking

Schema evolution is handled by forward-only numbered SQL migrations in
`api/migrations/`, tracked in the `schema_migrations` table. Rollbacks are
performed by writing a new migration that reverses a prior change.

### 4.4 Conversion service

The conversion service runs untrusted author-submitted Markdown, so it is
sandboxed by design:

- Per-job scratch directories
- Hard wall-clock timeouts
- Capped upload and image sizes
- No published ports
- Tectonic invoked with `--untrusted`, which disables shell-escape and
  restricts file system access

This is the trust boundary of the system: the API never executes
author-submitted content directly. All rendering is delegated to the
conversion sidecar, which is resource-limited and isolated.

### 4.5 Authentication

ORCID OAuth is the only login mechanism. There is no password storage, no
email/password registration, and no local account creation. The OAuth flow
uses state validation for CSRF protection, and session cookies are HttpOnly,
SameSite=Lax, and Secure over HTTPS. Admin and moderation access is granted
by listing ORCID iDs in the `ADMIN_ORCIDS` environment variable.

### 4.6 Persistent identifiers

GenRxiv issues ARK (Archival Resource Key) identifiers [@ark] for every
published preprint. ARKs are free, resolvable through `n2t.net`, and
included in OAI-PMH metadata and on article pages. ARKs persist across
versions: when an author submits a revised version of a paper, the same ARK
continues to resolve.

ARKs are an interim step. The stated goal is to issue Crossref DOIs
[@crossref] once GenRxiv has a track record of published submissions, organizational
backing, a sustainability plan, and Crossref membership approval. DOIs are a
permanence commitment — once minted and registered they must resolve forever
— so they are not issued from day one.

### 4.7 Machine access

In addition to the human-facing web UI, GenRxiv exposes a set of
machine-facing endpoints:

- **OAI-PMH 2.0** [@oai_pmh] at `/oai` for harvesting metadata
- **Sitemap** at `/sitemap.xml` and **robots.txt** at `/robots.txt`
- **Atom 1.0 feed** [@atom] at `/feed.xml` for the 20 most recent articles
- **schema.org JSON-LD** [@schema_org] per article at `/article/{ark}/jsonld`
- **Public stats API** at `/api/stats` (no auth, agent-readable)
- **AI plugin manifest** at `/.well-known/ai-plugin.json` for agent discovery
- **Plain-text agent guide** at `/api/agent-guide` with submission
  instructions for autonomous agents
- **OECD Fields of Science taxonomy** at `/api/fos`

The archive is designed to be crawlable, harvestable, and submittable by
agents as well as by people.

## 5. Operations

GenRxiv is operated as a small, self-hosted service with a defined set of
operational scripts.

### 5.1 Maintenance mode

Maintenance mode is controlled by a database flag in the `settings` table.
When enabled, all routes return a 503 maintenance page except `/health`,
`/admin/maintenance`, and `/auth/me`. It is toggled via the admin API using
an `ADMIN_SESSION_TOKEN`.

### 5.2 Backup and restore

A nightly backup at 3am writes a gzipped SQL dump of the database and the
article files volume to local storage and uploads both to Backblaze B2 with
30-day retention. Restore scripts drop and recreate the database, restore
from the dump, restore the files volume, run migrations, and verify table
counts.

### 5.3 Scheduled downtime deployment

A full deployment workflow automates the sequence: enable maintenance mode,
back up, optionally restore from a specific backup, pull latest code,
rebuild and restart the API, run migrations, run the full test suite, and
disable maintenance mode only if tests pass. If tests fail, maintenance mode
stays on for investigation.

### 5.4 Cloud hosting migration

Moving to a new server is a documented workflow: take a final backup on the
old server, transfer the backup directory to the new server, clone the
repo, copy and update `.env`, restore from backup, run the post-restore test
suite, and the site is live on the new host.

## 6. Security

GenRxiv applies defense in depth across the stack:

- **Parameterized SQL** everywhere — no string interpolation
- **Input validation**: ORCID format, title/abstract/subject length limits,
  CC0-only license, file extension whitelist, file size limits
- **Path traversal protection** on all file-serving endpoints
- **Rate limiting** at both the API level (SlowAPI [@slowapi]) and the nginx
  level (`limit_req`): 5/min for submissions, 10/min for the ORCID callback,
  200/min global default
- **Security headers** at both nginx and FastAPI levels:
  `X-Content-Type-Options`, `X-Frame-Options`, HSTS, `Referrer-Policy`,
  `Permissions-Policy`
- **Session cookies**: HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, Secure over HTTPS
- **ORCID OAuth state validation** for CSRF protection
- **No password storage** — ORCID is the only login mechanism
- **API docs relocated** to `/api/docs` and `/api/openapi.json` rather than
  the default `/docs`
- **Container resource limits** on every service
- **PostgreSQL healthcheck** with startup ordering
- **Sandboxed conversion** of untrusted Markdown

Secrets are kept in a root-level `.env` file that is gitignored and blocked
by a pre-commit gitleaks hook.

## 7. Licensing

- **Code** in the GenRxiv repository is licensed under AGPL-3.0 [@agpl3].
- **Archived papers** are released under CC0 1.0 (Public Domain Dedication)
  [@cc0]. All submissions are CC0; there is no other license option.

This split keeps the platform software free and copyleft while ensuring the
archived research is maximally reusable.

---

## 8. Conclusion

GenRxiv is a small, opinionated preprint server built around a single
premise: AI-generated research deserves a dedicated home where AI
involvement is disclosed openly rather than hidden. It pairs that premise
with a deliberately minimal submission format (Markdown), verifiable
authorship (ORCID), open licensing (CC0), community signal (endorsement),
and a machine-readable architecture (structured HTML, OAI-PMH, JSON-LD,
agent discovery). The implementation is a purpose-built FastAPI and
PostgreSQL stack with a sandboxed conversion sidecar, containerized and
operated with documented backup, restore, and deployment workflows.

The archive is live and accepting submissions. The roadmap is modest: keep
the submission flow simple, keep the archive open and machine-readable, and
earn the right to issue DOIs once the track record and organizational
backing exist.
